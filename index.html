Section 02 — Geography

Where does public transport reach?

Australia's vast geography creates stark contrasts in transport access. Urban centres enjoy dense networks while regional areas rely almost entirely on private vehicles.

Public transport commuter share by state (2021)

Percentage of employed residents who used public transport to get to work on Census day. NSW and VIC lead due to Sydney and Melbourne's rail networks.

How has transport changed?

Tracking shifts in how Australians commute between 2011 and 2021 — from the rise of working from home to the dramatic collapse of train ridership during the pandemic.

Commute mode over time (2011–2021)

Number of workers using each mode at each Census. The 2021 Census captured the pandemic's lasting shift toward remote work and away from public transport.

Source

ABS Census of Population and Housing 2011, 2016, 2021 — Method of Travel to Work.

Australian Bureau of Statistics — abs.gov.au/census

Public transport share: 2016 vs 2021 by state

Each line connects a state's public transport commuter share in 2016 to 2021. Downward slopes show how far ridership fell across all states during the pandemic period.

Source

Section 05 — Future

Towards greener transport

Australia's transition to sustainable transport is accelerating — from the rapid uptake of electric vehicles to government targets for decarbonising the transport sector.

EV registrations vs 2030 state targets

Current EV registrations (2024) shown against each state's indicative 2030 target. Bars show progress; the marker indicates the goal.

Source

EV registrations: Electric Vehicle Council, State of Electric Vehicles 2024.

2030 targets: State government EV strategies (NSW, VIC, QLD, WA, SA, ACT, TAS, NT). Targets are indicative where no legislated goal exists.

Green transport mode share (2018–2030)

Combined share of EV, public transport, walking and cycling. Dashed lines from 2025 onward indicate projected growth under current policy trajectories.

Source

Historical data (2018–2024): ABS Census 2021; Electric Vehicle Council State of EVs 2024; BITRE Australian Infrastructure Statistics Yearbook 2023.

Projections (2025–2030): modelled from current policy trajectories under the National Electric Vehicle Strategy (DCCEEW, 2023) — dcceew.gov.au
